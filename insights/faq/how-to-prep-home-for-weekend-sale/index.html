Your Blog Post Title Goes Right Here — Make It Count

A short subtitle or teaser sentence that hooks the reader and gives them a clear reason to keep scrolling. Keep it to one or two sentences.

RM
Ray Morandi
April 7, 2026
6 min read

This is the opening paragraph of your blog post. The first letter is styled as a drop cap automatically. Lead with your most compelling insight — give readers an immediate reason to trust you and keep reading. This paragraph should set up the central tension or question the article will answer.

Continue building context here. Provide the background your reader needs to appreciate the insight that follows. Use clear, direct language — your clients are smart people making major financial decisions, and they deserve straight talk.

Section Heading One

Use section headings to break up longer posts and let skimmers navigate to what matters most to them. Each section should deliver a complete, stand-alone idea.

$389K Median Sale Price
18 Avg. Days on Market
102% List-to-Sale Ratio
1.4 mo Months of Inventory

Follow up the data with your interpretation. Numbers alone don't tell the whole story — your expertise is what transforms raw statistics into actionable advice for buyers and sellers.

Section Heading Two

Another key point. Use bullet lists sparingly — most ideas deserve a full sentence or paragraph. But when you have a clear list of distinct items, a bulleted list is faster to scan:

  • First important item with enough detail to be useful
  • Second item — don't pad, but don't truncate either
  • Third item that rounds out the list logically
  • A fourth item if it genuinely adds value

Return to prose after the list to tie the points together and lead into the next idea.

"In a market with less than two months of inventory, sellers who price strategically from day one are consistently netting more than those who test the market high and reduce later." — Ray Morandi, Licensed IL Real Estate Broker

Section Heading Three

Use this section for tactical advice — the specific, actionable steps your reader can take based on what you've shared above. This is where you earn trust by demonstrating real expertise, not just market awareness.

Sub-point A

A more granular point within the section. Use H3 headings sparingly — only when a section is long enough to need internal structure.

Sub-point B

Another tactical sub-point. Keep these concrete and specific. Vague advice ("work with a professional") is less useful than precise guidance ("request a comparative market analysis that includes expired listings, not just recent sales").
