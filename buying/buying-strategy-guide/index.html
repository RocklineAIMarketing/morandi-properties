The Home Buying Process

What to Expect — Step by Step

01
Step 01 Week 1

Get Pre-Approved for a Mortgage

Before you look at a single listing, get a mortgage pre-approval letter from a lender. This tells you exactly how much home you can afford and shows sellers you are a serious, qualified buyer. In a competitive market like Orland Park or Tinley Park, homes move fast — sometimes within 24 to 48 hours of hitting the MLS. Showing up without pre-approval means you will almost certainly lose out to a buyer who did their homework.

Ray works with a network of trusted local lenders who know the southwest suburbs market and can turn pre-approvals around quickly.

02
Step 02 Week 1–2

Define Your Needs & Choose a Neighborhood

The southwest suburbs each have a distinct character. Orland Park offers excellent schools, walkable retail, and strong resale value. Frankfort and Mokena have a quieter, small-town feel with newer construction options. Tinley Park offers strong commuter access and a wide range of price points. New Lenox is one of the fastest-growing communities in Will County. Getting this right before you start touring homes saves weeks of searching.

Ray has lived in the southwest suburbs his entire life and can walk you through the real differences between every neighborhood, subdivision, and school district in the area.

03
Step 03 Week 2–4

Search Listings & Tour Homes

With your criteria set, you will start touring active listings. Your agent will set up MLS alerts so you see new homes the moment they hit the market. Pay close attention to layout, lot, condition, and location relative to schools and major roads — not just finishes, which are easy to change. A home that checks every surface box but backs up to a busy road or sits in a flood zone can be a costly mistake.

The best homes in desirable southwest suburb school districts often go under contract within a week. Be ready to move quickly when the right one appears.

04
Step 04 Day of Decision

Make a Competitive Offer

When you find the right home, your agent will pull comparable sales and help you craft an offer that is competitive without overpaying. In a seller's market, this may mean offering at or above list price, limiting contingencies, or writing an escalation clause. The terms of an offer — closing timeline, earnest money, contingencies — matter just as much as the number. A well-structured offer at list price often beats a higher offer with messy terms.

Ray has helped clients win multiple-offer situations across the southwest suburbs for 20 years. His strategy is built on knowing exactly what local sellers respond to.

05
Step 05 Within 5–7 Days of Accepted Offer

Home Inspection

After your offer is accepted, you will hire a licensed home inspector to evaluate the property top to bottom — roof, foundation, electrical, plumbing, HVAC, and more. The report identifies any material defects. From there you can negotiate repairs or credits with the seller, walk away if issues are serious enough, or accept the home as-is with full knowledge of what you are taking on. Never waive the inspection unless you fully understand the risk.

Older homes in established Orland Park and Tinley Park neighborhoods can come with aging mechanicals. A thorough inspection protects your investment before you commit.

06
Step 06 Weeks 2–4 Under Contract

Appraisal & Underwriting

Your lender will order an appraisal to confirm the home's market value supports the purchase price. If the appraisal comes in low, you will need to renegotiate the price, cover the gap in cash, or walk away. Simultaneously, underwriting will verify your income, assets, credit, and employment. This is when lenders typically request additional documentation — respond quickly to keep your closing on schedule.

Avoid any major financial changes during this phase — new credit cards, large purchases, or job changes — until after the keys are in your hand.

07
Step 07 30–45 Days After Accepted Offer

Final Walk-Through & Closing Day

A day or two before closing, you will walk through the home one final time to confirm it is in agreed-upon condition and that any negotiated repairs were completed. Then comes closing day — you will sign loan documents, pay your closing costs and down payment, and receive the keys. In Illinois, closings take place at a title company. The full process from accepted offer to keys typically runs 30 to 45 days.

Morandi Properties stays with you through every detail of closing — reviewing the settlement statement, coordinating with the title company, and making sure nothing falls through the cracks.

Common Questions

Buyers Ask. Ray Answers.

From the day you start your search to closing day, most buyers in the southwest Chicago suburbs are in their new home within 60 to 90 days. The process breaks down into two phases: the search (which can take anywhere from a few weeks to a few months depending on inventory and how quickly you act) and the contract-to-close period, which typically runs 30 to 45 days. Getting pre-approved before you start searching is the single best way to compress that timeline.

You are not legally required to have a buyer's agent in Illinois, but working without one means negotiating directly against a seller's agent whose job is to protect the seller — not you. A buyer's agent costs you nothing out of pocket (the seller typically pays both commissions), and a good one will negotiate harder, spot red flags earlier, and save you far more than any fee involved. In a competitive market like Orland Park or Tinley Park, having an experienced local agent is a significant strategic advantage.

The common benchmark is 20% down, which lets you avoid private mortgage insurance (PMI) and typically qualifies you for better rates. That said, many buyers in Illinois purchase with significantly less — FHA loans allow as little as 3.5% down, and conventional loans can go as low as 3% for qualified buyers. In a multiple-offer situation, a stronger down payment can make your offer more attractive to sellers even if the price is the same. Ray's lending network can walk you through the options that make sense for your specific financial situation.

It depends entirely on what matters most to you. Orland Park consistently tops the list for school quality, retail access, and long-term resale strength. Frankfort and Mokena appeal to buyers who want a quieter, small-town feel with newer construction options. Tinley Park offers excellent Metra access and a wider price range. New Lenox is growing fast and offers strong value for buyers who don't mind being slightly further out. Ray has spent 20 years working in all five communities and can give you an honest, street-level comparison based on your priorities.

The southwest suburbs remain one of the more competitive residential real estate markets in the Chicago metro area. Well-priced homes in good school districts routinely receive multiple offers within the first few days on market, and above-asking-price sales are common. Inventory has remained tight, which continues to favor sellers. As a buyer, that means being pre-approved, moving quickly, and having an experienced agent who can structure a compelling offer is more important than ever. Ray monitors market conditions across all five communities in real time.
